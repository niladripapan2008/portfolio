02 — About

Chasing the same ten minutes, in different parts of the world

I started shooting on a borrowed camera on a fishing boat off the Icelandic coast, waiting for a storm to clear. What held me wasn't the storm — it was the half hour after, when the sky turns a color that has no proper name.

That's most of what I photograph now: the narrow window where a place is neither day nor night. It asks for patience more than equipment — long waits, longer exposures, and a willingness to come back to the same cliff four evenings in a row.

03 — Approach

How a shoot comes together

Whether it's a personal series or a commission, the process stays the same.

Scout

Reading the light first

Before any camera comes out, I spend a day walking the location at the hour I intend to shoot it, watching how the light actually moves rather than how a map says it should.

Wait

Shooting in available light

No artificial light, no filters that alter what was there. A long exposure and a steady tripod do the work that editing shouldn't have to.

Print

Finishing by hand

Every print is colour-graded and proofed by hand in small batches, so the blue in the file is the blue that reaches the wall.
